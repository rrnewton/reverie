# Entry-branch replay established; diagnostic refusal preserved

This is analysis of the one authorized V2 observation, not a new run or an independent source approval. The unchanged test failed again with raw 101 at `clock_origin_tests.rs:308`: actual `[3, 67]`, required `[1, 65]`. Its earlier `post_exec=1` assertion passed, but the trace establishes that this one count came from backend execution and rewind of the entry instruction, not forward guest progress. The two extra counts at the first syscall boundary are the two erroneous entry steps associated with private Mprotect and Tool Getpid injection.

The observation wrapper remains raw 125 / accepted=false. Its unchanged reader requires an authenticated mapped perf-page snapshot and obtained none. `DIAGNOSTIC.json` still says `RuntimeError('no authenticated stopped-target clock snapshot')`. This report uses the independently retained successful kernel `read(fd)` results and register/stop trace; it does not change that refusal or claim a complete diagnostic under the original reader contract.

## What establishes execution and rewind

1. After PTRACE_EVENT_EXEC and the extra exec SIGTRAP, GETREGSET 222 still reports RIP `0x401000`. Preinitialization temporarily installs a syscall there for mmap. Successful PTRACE_POKEDATA entry 250 / return 252 restores the numeric word `0x0f00000027b80075` at `0x401000`. Little-endian bytes are `75 00 b8 27 00 00 00 0f`: `JNE +0`, then `MOV EAX,39`, then the first byte of the following syscall. Thus the instruction subsequently stepped is authenticated from a real successful write, not only inferred from the fixture source.
2. Before Mprotect, SETREGSET 262 sets `orig_rax=-1` while RIP remains `0x401000`. SINGLESTEP 266 succeeds, and waitid 270 returns SIGTRAP for the guest. SETREGSET 271 restores RIP `0x401000` and the original registers. There is **no actual GETREGSET between that trap and the restoration**. GETREGSET 277 then confirms the restored entry RIP. The private Mprotect runs at `0x71000000`; GETREGSET 292 directly reports successful result zero and RIP `0x71000002`. The original entry context is restored again. Raw counter read 308 is one; GETREGSET 312 is still `0x401000`.
3. The post-exec Tool Getpid follows the same wrong path: SETREGSET 314 at entry with `orig_rax=-1`, SINGLESTEP 318, SIGTRAP 322, then SETREGSET 323 restores entry RIP. Again there is **no direct post-skip RIP sample before that restoration**. The private Getpid starts at `0x71000000` and GETREGSET 342 reports the exact guest PID 2578706 at `0x71000002`. SETREGSET 348 restores entry RIP.
4. The ordinary post-exec step is then SINGLESTEP 352 / SIGTRAP 356. This time GETREGSET 359 directly shows RIP `0x401002`, after the entry conditional branch. The guest proceeds to the first real Getpid; GETREGSET 370 reports seccomp RIP `0x401009`, and raw read 378 reports three. At the second Getpid, GETREGSET 405 reports `0x401019` and raw read 414 reports 67.

The successful step/trap from authenticated `JNE +0`, followed by successful entry-RIP restoration, establishes the replay mechanism. The ordinary final branch has an actual post-step RIP sample; the two erroneous skip branches have step/trap plus instruction-byte and restoration evidence. They must not be described as having direct post-step RIP samples. Cumulative deltas 0→1 and 1→3 are directly measured. Assigning one increment to each skip and one to the legitimate entry step follows from those intervals plus the complete reachable instruction sequence and source; there is no directly sampled intermediate value two.

The unchanged fixture then executes exactly 64 conditional loop branches between Getpid boundaries, matching the measured 67−3=64. Its private syscall trampoline contains syscall/UD2 instructions, not conditional branches. Those private-page instruction bytes are source-bound rather than separately captured: the observation did not intercept the private-page process-memory write. The actual private RIP transitions and syscall results are directly captured. This distinction does not leave a competing conditional-branch path in the inspected source, but it limits what the trace alone proves.

The runtime-created guest fixture file was removed by its normal owner. Its byte builder and retained test ELF are bound; this packet does not claim a separate retained guest ELF. The restored first eight entry bytes are independently present in the actual POKEDATA record.

## Source cause and scope

`task.rs::skip_seccomp_syscall` explicitly requires an actual seccomp stop with RIP after the syscall instruction. It sets the syscall number to −1, single-steps, then restores the supplied register context. `private_inject` calls it unconditionally. Both `inner_inject` and `inner_tail_inject` route a missing pending syscall and a different still-pending syscall to that helper after `pending_syscall.take()`. At successful exec, `pending_syscall` is cleared. Therefore Mprotect during preinitialization and Getpid in the post-exec hook take this wrong no-pending path. At entry they execute a guest instruction rather than consume a kernel syscall-entry stop, then rewind RIP. Restoring registers does not undo a retired branch or a possible memory side effect.

There is a separate reachable stale-state defect in the same lifecycle: the ordinary intercepted emulated-return epilogue calls `skip_seccomp_syscall` while `pending_syscall` is Some, but does not clear that Some afterward. Later signal/timer/instruction callbacks can therefore see a logical pending record that no longer represents an active seccomp stop. A patch that only tests Some versus None without fixing this epilogue would be incomplete. `PROPOSAL.md` maps the full relevant lifecycle and proposes the smallest correction and discriminating controls; it is not implemented here.

This proves the specific extra branch replay in the first `injected_exec=false` mode. It does not establish behavior of the later injected-exec mode, timer variants, other declarations, other architectures or all historical parity differences. The original corrected component run still has one accepted test and one failed test; the later seven declarations and the second mode of this failed declaration remain unqualified. This diagnostic attempt is not an additional passing qualification.

## Diagnostic and operational limits

All 160 recorded direct perf-page observations failed with `MemoryError('Cannot access memory at address 0x7f9967b35000')`; 142 were at points the observer classified as stopped. No page sequence/index/offset snapshot is authenticated. The cause of GDB's inability to read that mapping is not established here. There is no counter sample immediately after either erroneous step, and ioctl operations were not traced; absence of resets follows from the inspected clock lifecycle, not an ioctl transcript. No retry is proposed merely to satisfy the original reader.

The test itself failed in 0.161539273 seconds. GDB returned 101 in 0.502546342 seconds without forced termination. Wrapper payload elapsed 1.242659664 seconds; complete service accounting was 5.353165 CPU seconds and 6.751878184 observer wall seconds. No timeout or observer error occurred. The service ended inactive/dead with MainPID zero, empty ControlGroup and complete accounting. The fresh observation lease has an ordinary failed-phase completion. The original metadata lease remains unresolved and unchanged; its exclusion was held during the observation and verified during final readback, with no recovery or token mutation.

The first GDB 9 setup refusal and every capability-query failure remain bound. One exploratory display of the large setup/module record and a multi-file source batch were truncated. Focused reads repaired the concrete source and trace fields used for this analysis. The entire raw trace and setup remain hashed; no claim of model-visible completeness is made for every exploratory module-list rendering. All launch sessions, including observation session 89104, are collected and terminal.

No assertion was weakened, tolerance widened, case skipped, comparator relaxed, failure relabelled as a pass, or check deleted. The original post-exec expectation of one remains in the unchanged failed source; the observation refutes its claim that this count represents legitimate forward guest work. Any future correction to that temporal oracle requires an explicit reviewed successor that also proves preserved entry RIP and untouched guest memory, while retaining the real first-branch and loop counts exactly. No fixed subtraction, reset or count offset is proposed.
